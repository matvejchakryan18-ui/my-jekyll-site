-ABRAXAS-

“In any unclear situation — experiment.”

This quote became the starting point of the project — a way to move beyond imitation and construct a distinct visual identity within the phonk scene.

From Imitation to Identity

This project explores the creation of a distinct visual identity within the phonk genre. It began as a personal study of drift phonk aesthetics and evolved into a concept-driven album cover rooted in symbolism and historical references.

Year: 2025
Type: Personal project, phonk cover brand
Role: Concept, art direction, typography, visual design
Deliverables: Cover artwork, icons, VHS-style assets

Copying Oth ers Only Gets You So Far

Early works relied on imitation of existing artists such as KSLV and gqtis. While useful for understanding the genre, they lacked a distinct identity. The goal became to move beyond replication and construct a visual language that reflects both personal interests and the expectations of the phonk audience.

Designing for th e Occult 

The project targets the esoteric underground segment of the phonk community. This audience is drawn to occult symbolism, obscurity, and VHS-based nostalgia. Defining this context allowed the concept to move from personal expression to intentional communication.

A Gnostic Demon Walks Into a Phonk Cover

The concept developed through an interest in mythology, demonology, and obscure internet media. I selected Abraxas — a Gnostic symbol of duality — as the central figure. This choice allowed the design to balance opposing forces: beauty and distortion, control and chaos. Early concepts explored direct imitation of drift phonk visuals, but these were rejected due to lack of identity.

Resolution

The visual language draws from late 19th-century Art Nouveau. I used peacock feathers, an androgynous figure, and Cinzel Decorative to construct a symbolic system. References to Alphonse Mucha and Aubrey Beardsley informed the balance between ornament and contrast. Analog distortion and VHS textures connect these influences to contemporary phonk aesthetics. The color palette reinforces contrast and tension, while the typeface adds a ceremonial and expressive tone.

The Birth of an entity

The final design positions the work within a niche segment of the phonk scene, offering a more symbolic and identity-driven alternative to typical genre visuals. The concept is translated into a format suitable for album covers and digital distribution, aligning with the visual expectations of the phonk scene.

Community Feedback

When shared within the Phonk Nation Discord community, the cover was recognized for its distinct atmosphere compared to typical drift visuals. One user described it as: “This feels like phonk but from another dimension — darker, more symbolic.” This response confirmed that the direction resonated with the intended audience while standing apart stylistically.

What I Would Push Further Next

Building Abraxas taught me that a personal brand is not a style, it is a position. The occult register, the Art Nouveau tension, the Cyrillic lettering on the vinyl label all have to pull in the same direction or the identity dissolves. While the symbolic system is consistent, the depth and spatial hierarchy could be further refined to improve clarity and impact. Shadows and depth remain the unfinished work. -FAMUFEST 40-

Year: 2023
Type: Client project, film festival visual identity
Team: Jasmin Linhardtová, Nina Kučerová, Diana Huong Nguyen, Matvey Chakryan
Role: Web layout design
Deliverables: Web layout, mobile mockup

Point of departure

Famufest is a multi-genre film festival presenting new cinematic voices to a wider audience. For the 40th edition, our team built the concept around immersion — the moment the viewer is drawn into the image. The project was developed collaboratively (visual identity with Jasmin Linhardtová, Nina Kučerová, Diana Huong Nguyen). I designed the poster as the main visual entry point and translated the identity into the website layout.

Tension

The identity needed to communicate clearly while suggesting a loss of control. I explored how far I could pull the viewer into a cinematic state without losing readability. I decided to focus on the moment where the viewer transitions from observer to participant. At the same time, I ensured the system worked across formats — from poster to web.

Visual language

I constructed the composition around the relationship between the screen and the viewer. I used the television as a symbol of the film industry and positioned the figure in a moment of transition. I developed the poster using dark tones, strong contrast, and chrome textures to create a controlled, hypnotic atmosphere. Before arriving at this direction, I explored a more colorful and warm variant with a softer, liquid logo. I abandoned it because the increased number of colors weakened focus and diluted the core idea of immersion. I then applied the final visual language to the web layout, maintaining consistency across formats. The color palette reinforces contrast and tension, while Oswald provides a condensed, cinematic presence.

Resolution

The result is a visual proposal spanning print and digital applications. I translated the core idea of immersion into both the poster and the website, creating a consistent visual system. Although the concept was not selected, it demonstrates how a single idea can remain legible while moving across formats and constraints.

What the Web Taught Me That the Poster Couldn’t

Working across poster and web helped me understand how visual identity behaves in different mediums. I improved my ability to balance expressive design with clarity and usability. The project also showed me that constraints — such as format and production — are part of the design process itself. In future iterations, I would explore more variations before finalizing the composition.
