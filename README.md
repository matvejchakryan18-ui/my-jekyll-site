# Portfolio

-ABRAXAS-

From Imitation to Identity

My entry into the phonk design space began with imitation. I studied the visual language of drift phonk artists such as KSLV to understand the foundations of the genre. These works became a starting point, but also revealed the need for a more distinct and personal direction.

Defining th e Audience

The project targets the esoteric underground segment of the phonk community. This audience is drawn to occult symbolism, obscurity, and nostalgic VHS aesthetics. Understanding this context allowed me to connect personal interests with a specific visual culture.

Constructing Abraxas

The concept emerged from an interest in mythology, demonology, and obscure internet media. I chose Abraxas — a Gnostic symbol of duality — as the central figure. This allowed the design to balance opposing forces: beauty and distortion, control and chaos.     

«In any unclear situation, Experiment!»

It was this quote from Soviet animator Boris Stepantsev that became my design direction «In any unclear situation, experiment!». The chosen tools were attributes of late 19th-century Art Nouveau.  Peacock feathers, androgynous figure, the Cinzel Decorative typeface – all of this is reflected in the visauls and language. The warm patterns of Alphonse Mucha and the cool images of Aubrey Beardsley also played a role.

Visual Language

The visual direction draws from late 19th-century Art Nouveau.I incorporated peacock feathers, an androgynous figure, and the Cinzel Decorative typeface to build a symbolic system. Influences from Alphonse Mucha and Aubrey Beardsley shaped the balance between ornament and contrast.Analog distortion and VHS textures connect these references to contemporary phonk aesthetics. The color palette reinforces contrast and tension, while Cinzel Decorative adds a ceremonial, expressive tone to the typography.

Resolution

The final outcome presents Abraxas as a symbolic entity — positioned between mythology and contemporary visual culture. The design translates into formats suitable for digital platforms and physical media, allowing it to exist within the phonk visual environment.

Observations

This project clarified the role of personal branding in visual design. I developed a stronger understanding of how symbolic systems create identity. At the same time, I identified areas for improvement, particularly in depth and spatial clarity.



-FAMUFEST 40-

Point of departure

The idea of immersion — the moment the viewer is drawn into the image — became the starting point for FAMUFEST 40. FAMUFEST is a multi-genre film festival presenting new cinematic voices to a wider audience. The project was developed collaboratively (visual identity with Jasmin Linhardtová, Nina Kučerová, Diana Huong Nguyen). I was responsible for the poster and for translating the identity into the website layout.

Tension

The identity needed to communicate clearly while suggesting a loss of control. It had to present information, yet pull the viewer into a cinematic state.I explored how far the image could push immersion without losing readability. At the same time, the system had to function across print and digital formats.

Visual language

I built the visual language around the relationship between the screen and the observer. The television symbolizes the film industry, while the viewer is placed in a moment of transition. I used dark tones, strong contrast, and chrome textures to create a hypnotic, contemporary atmosphere. The color palette reinforces tension, while Oswald adds a condensed, cinematic presence.

Resolution

The result is a visual proposal spanning poster and digital applications. I translated the core idea of immersion into the website layout, maintaining consistency across formats. Although not selected as the final identity, the project demonstrates a cohesive system built on a single concept.

What i learned

I learned how a single visual idea can extend across multiple formats. The project improved my ability to balance expressive design with clarity and usability. Working on both poster and web strengthened my understanding of hierarchy and rhythm.In future iterations, I would explore more variations before finalizing the composition.
